# Re-check — 2026-09-17, after judging

Two defects found by the judges of Clief Notes Competition 12, in their letter on the entry
judged at commit `d0b2b3c`. Neither was found by a run or by the author. This page records what
was wrong, how each fix was proved, and what the corrected checks say about every ledger already

## 1 · The standard's text held words the PDF does not

**What was wrong.** `reference/` was extracted with pypdf. `SOURCES.md` said the split words in it
(`s hould`, `Program me`) were the PDF's own kerning and kept them "exactly as extracted". The
judges measured the glyph boxes and found no gap and no span boundary inside those words: the
PDF prints `should` and `Programme`.

**Confirmed before changing anything,** on the vendored PDF (SHA-256 unchanged,
`efa748d7…34184`):

| Extractor | `s hould` | `Program me` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pypdf (built the old `reference/`; 6.12.1 is what is installed now) | 1 | 5 |
| pdftotext (poppler 26.04.0) | 0 | 0 |
| pdfplumber 0.11.9 | 0 | 0 |

**What pypdf got wrong in total** (old section files against new, word for word):

- 9 words split: `s hould`, `s ame`, `Program me` ×5, `Progra mme`, `Incu bation`.
  (`SOURCES.md` also listed `Particip ant`. It was not in the old files.)
- 17 spaces added before a hyphen, colon or comma: `high -potential`, `Organizer :`,
  `incorporation , unless`, and so on.
- The last two rows of Art. 3's table (criteria 5–6) were placed inside § III Art. 6.3.
  **Checked against the page image:** on PDF page 5 they come before Art. 4 and Section III.
  They now sit in `02-mandatory-elegible-criteria.md`. The old `SOURCES.md` said this split
  "follows the PDF's pages", which was wrong.
- The Art. 12 timeline table was placed in § VII, leaving `06-timeline.md` a heading with no body.
  Two blind runs reported that on 2026-09-11. Nobody traced it to the extractor.
- Rows 3–5 of the Art. 6.5 scoring table were placed inside Art. 6.7.

**The fix.** All eleven section files re-extracted with pdfplumber, tables written one cell at a
time, and checked word for word, page by page, against pdftotext. The two tools disagree in two
places only, and in both the page decides:

- pdftotext joins four words hyphenated across a line break (`nondiscriminatory`). The page
  prints `non-` at the end of a line.
- pdftotext drops the five dashes that open the Art. 10 list. The page prints them (checked on
  the page image).

`ELEGIBLE` and `JURISDICITION` are unchanged, as are the publisher's other errors. Curly
apostrophes: 9 in the PDF (8 in the stored sections, 1 on the cover), plus one straight
apostrophe in Annex 1 Art. 20.3(c). All kept as printed.

**How it is proved.** `python3 _tools/check-extraction.py` compares every stored word, page by
page, with both extractors, and fails on any word that neither reads.

| Text checked | Result |
| --- | --- |
| The new section files | every page ok. Disagreements between the two extractors printed for a person to check: the four hyphens and five dashes above |
| The old pypdf section files, from `d0b2b3c` | **8 of the 14 text pages fail**, and every split word and stray space is listed |

**What this check cannot see:** order. It counts words per page, so a table moved to the wrong place on the same page passes. The Art. 12 timeline moved from § VI to § VII without leaving page 11, and the word check passes it. Table placement was checked against the page images, by eye.

`SOURCES.md` quotes its old claim and says plainly that it was wrong, and how that was found.
`LOADING.md` step 2 now requires two extractors and a look at the page where they disagree.
`examples.md` had two quotes carrying the artefact; both corrected, each with a dated note.

## 2 · The citation check ignored the provision number

**What was wrong.** `_tools/check-citations.py` searched the whole of `reference/` for a quote.
The judges changed a citation from § II Art. 3(4) to 3(3), and then to 3(9), which does not exist,
leaving the quote alone. Both passed. `rules.md` § 2 anchors a row to the provision **and** the
quote, and only the quote was being checked.

**The fix.** `reference/PROVISIONS.md` maps 83 provisions by hand, each to the exact stretch of
the section file that holds it: a start string that must appear exactly once, and an end string.
The map is written out row by row, not derived from filenames or page numbers. This document
repeats a clause number (6.4), misnumbers an article ("Article 59") and gives Article 16 the
clause numbers 15.1 and 15.2. The checker now:

- reads the provision number in front of each quote, including a second quote under a second
  number on the same row;
- fails a number that is not in the map, and never falls back to a shorter one: `§ II Art. 3(9)`
  is not `§ II Art. 3`;
- fails a quote that is in the standard but not in the cited provision, and **prints where it
  actually is**;
- fails a label that is not a printed number, instead of quietly filing its quote under the
  provision before it;
- refuses to run at all (exit 2) if the map is broken;
- removes the `[pN]` page markers before comparing. The old checker failed a correct quote that
  ran over a page break, because the marker sat in the middle of it.

**How it is proved.** `python3 _tools/test-citations.py`: nine cases in
`_tools/tests/citations/`, each declaring the exit code **and** the words its output must
contain, so a case that fails for the wrong reason also fails the test. After those, four ways of
breaking the map, each in a throwaway copy.

| Case | New checker | Old checker (`d0b2b3c`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| neighbour swap: Art. 3(4)'s quote cited to 3(3) | fails, "it is in § II Art. 3(4)" | **passed** |
| non-existent provision: Art. 3(9) | fails, names 3(9) | **passed** |
| an article cited to the wrong section: § II Art. 7 | fails | **passed** |
| criterion 5 cited to § III Art. 6.3, where pypdf had put it | fails, "it is in § II Art. 3(5)" | **passed** |
| a second quote under a label that is not a number | fails | **passed** |
| the split word `s hould` | fails | failed |
| a straight apostrophe for the source's curly one | fails | failed |
| correct citations, in both ledger row shapes | passes | passed |
| a correct quote over a page break; criterion 5 on page 5; a parent citing a child's text | passes | **failed** (page marker) |
| broken map: missing start text / repeated start text / wrong file / end before start | refuses, exit 2 | no map |

Result: **13 tests, 0 failed.**

## Added later on 2026-09-17: the `UNREADABLE` boundary, settled without a re-run

*Added, not edited.*

Decided by Iris: **a file that is there but cannot be opened or read is `UNREADABLE`, never
`NOT SUPPLIED`, and so is any requirement whose material is inside it.** `NOT SUPPLIED` is only
for a document the standard requires that is not in the package at all. Every `UNREADABLE` row names the file and gives a
reason, `CANNOT OPEN` or `ILLEGIBLE`, because the fix is different: send it again unlocked, or
send a better copy.

Written into `rules.md` § 9, the row shape and self-audit 2a. **Not re-tested blind**, chosen
knowingly. It writes down what the answer key already expected and blind case 8 already did, and
the only fixture it can change is `broken-11-encrypted`, whose expected row now also asks for
`CANNOT OPEN`. No fixture exercises `ILLEGIBLE`.

Corrected in the same pass: `package/CONTEXT.md` and `fixtures/broken-09-scanned/README.md`
still described an image-only scan as `UNREADABLE`, from before the 2026-09-11 redefinition. The
row shape in `rules.md` listed every verdict except `UNREADABLE`. The README said `UNREADABLE`
had never fired, when case 8 had fired it.
