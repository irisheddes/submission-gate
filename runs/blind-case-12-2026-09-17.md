## What this run found about the repository

1. **The answer key cites a rule that does not exist.** `EXPECTED.md` says `rules.md` § 7
   requires `TO CHECK` for a page count or duration that is declared rather than measured. § 7
   says nothing of the kind: `TO CHECK` is for a provision not re-read, or one whose scope is
   ambiguous. Cases 11 and 12 both returned `R-08` `CONFIRMED`, and both applied § 7 as written.
2. **`REQUIREMENTS.md` misquotes Art. 3(3).** Its `R-03b` row says "Medium **or** High". The
   standard prints "Medium **and** High". The run quoted the section, which takes precedence.
3. **The citation check rejects a correct form of citation.** The run cited provisions the way
   the standard prints them, e.g. "SECTION II, Article 3, criterion 4 (Company Size)", not
   "§ II Art. 3(4)". `check-citations.py` fails 13 of its 14 citations as unmapped labels, and
   for every one prints that the quote is in exactly the provision the run named. All 14 quotes
   are verbatim, curly apostrophes included: **no tidying at all**. Case 11 tidied 3 of 12.
   `rules.md` asks for "the section number as printed on the standard" and never fixes a
   notation; the `§` form comes from `REQUIREMENTS.md` and `examples.md`, and this run could not
   see `examples.md`.

**[R-05] · The company belongs to one of the priority sectors**
- Verdict:    FAIL
- Severity:   NON-CONFORMANT
- Confidence: TO CHECK
- Provision:  SECTION II, Article 3, criterion 5 (Sector Priority) — "The Company should belong to one of the AI Factory’s priority sectors: Health, Agriculture & Climate, Finance & Legal, Policy Making & Public Administration, Communication & Media, Energy."
  Read with SECTION II, Article 4 (Discretionary Consideration) — "Applicants operating outside the defined strategic sectors may be considered, provided they demonstrate intensive use, development or investment of AI."
  And with SECTION III, Article 6, 6.3 — "Only applications that fully meet such criteria shall be considered eligible applications and proceed to the selection stage."
- Checked in: `registration-form.md` § 3 Sector (primary sector declared; "Secondary relevance: none"). `executive-summary.md` Page 4 is consistent with it.
- What falls short: The declared sector is not one of the six listed in criterion 5.
- Why it is not BLOCKING, and why it is TO CHECK: Criterion 5 says *should*. Article 4 lets an applicant outside the sectors be considered at the Organizer's discretion, provided it demonstrates intensive AI use. The package meets that condition (R-03, intensive AI use and development). But Article 4 says "may be considered", which gives the Organizer discretion. It does not make the application compliant, so § 5a does not turn this row into a PASS. The standard pulls two ways. The Article 3 preamble calls every criterion a "mandatory requirement". Article 6.3 admits only applications that "fully meet" Article 3. Criterion 5 is written as *should*, and Article 4 opens a discretionary route. The auditor does not pick a side. *Check with the Organizer's contact point:* is an applicant outside the priority sectors that meets Article 4's condition an eligible application under Article 6.3?

## 6 · What was not checked, and why

- **The presentation video itself.** Only a description of it reached this run, so its existence
  and its length (R-09, the one-minute video) are unverified.
- **The Executive Summary as a PDF.** The page count for R-08, the five-page limit, comes from
  the document's own page divisions in the text supplied. The submitted A4 layout was not part of
  this run.
- **The One-Stop Shop User Journey readiness levels.** The standard points to an external page
  that is not in `reference/`, so R-03b, the readiness level, cannot be confirmed from this
  folder.
- **Whether a sector miss that meets Article 4's condition is eligible under Article 6.3.** The
  standard does not settle it (R-05, sector priority).
- **What "a signed copy of this document" covers**, and whether the signature is authentic and
  the signatory had authority to sign. The auditor cannot verify either (R-11, the signed Rules).
- **The truth of declared facts.** Incorporation, the tax number, headcount and the in-person
  commitment are declarations of facts that could be verified. SECTION III, Article 6.3 gives the
  verifying to the Organizer. The auditor checked that each is present, specific and not
  contradicted elsewhere in the package. It did not check registries.
- **Whether this is the whole package.** The auditor cannot tell a document that was not supplied
  from one that does not exist. The README.md each file refers to was not supplied, and nothing
  in the standard requires it.
- **Timing.** SECTION VI, Article 12 sets the application period as 15 July to 15 September 2026
  (23:59h). It places no obligation on the applicant, so it has no row. The package states a
  submission date inside that period. This audit was run after the period closed.
- **The auditor's own calibration.** `_tools/status.py`, `fixtures/` and `examples.md` are named
  in `CLAUDE.md` but are not in this folder. The fixture regression and the automated citation
  check could not be run. The SHA-256 was checked by hand instead, and it matches.
